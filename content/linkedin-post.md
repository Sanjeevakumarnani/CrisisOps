CrisisOps is a disaster-response memory engine built around one simple idea: emergency teams should not have to rediscover what previous responders already learned.

It turns field reports into a common operational picture covering affected people, needs, resources, responders, transport constraints, local conditions and attempted actions.

Then the memory becomes part of the next decision.

Capture → Retain → Recall → Reflect → Human decision → Outcome → Future memory.

The interface makes the difference visible: a generic response baseline is compared with a memory-backed review that can surface similar situations, recurring bottlenecks, resource conflicts and transport constraints.

The system is designed as decision support, not autonomous dispatch. The demo uses synthetic data and keeps human confirmation at the centre.

The interesting part is not simply storing history. It is making history useful at the exact moment an operational decision is being reviewed.
